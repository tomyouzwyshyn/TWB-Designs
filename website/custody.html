Chain of custody

The file closes without a gap in it.

Custody is not a feature of the machine. It is the reason to buy the machine.

What we commit to.

  • No transfer. The material does not leave your controlled area between the moment it is signed out and the moment it ceases to exist.
  • Your operators. We train your people. TWB personnel are not required for routine operation and are never custodians of your material.
  • No resale, no diversion. Recovered metal is graded and weighed on site under your observation. Nothing recovered from seized or surrendered property is resold as a component or a part.
  • Certificate of destruction. Issued per cycle against your own inventory identifiers, in a form built to attach directly to your property record.
  • Auditable. The protocol is written, versioned, and available for your internal audit or inspector general review before you buy anything.
Why this clause exists

The resale problem

In January 2024 the Michigan State Police suspended a firearms-destruction contract after it emerged that the contractor was reselling salvaged parts from destroyed weapons.

Destruction vendors who monetise recovered components have a structural conflict with the agencies they serve. We publish the no-resale commitment first, in writing, because it is the question a property custodian should ask and rarely gets answered.

The economics still work — because our recovered value comes from graded metal, never from parts. The incentive points the same way yours does.

Status. The formal chain-of-custody and certificate-of-destruction protocol is being documented for independent audit. In preparation

Remote operation

Your people, at the panel. Nobody at the chamber.

Vault runs on remote start and remote stop, with interlocked controls and over-pressure relief. During a cycle the operators are at standoff distance — the protocol has no step that puts a person next to live ordnance.
