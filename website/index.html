The problem

Every transfer is a window. Most disposal chains are made of them.

The standard method for destroying ammunition in 2026 is to put it in a hole in the ground and set it on fire. The standard alternative is to hand it to a contractor and take their word for it.

Both routes move the material out of your control. A manifest is written. A truck leaves. Somebody else's people handle rounds that are still, on paper, your responsibility. Poorly secured and surplus stockpiles are a documented source of diverted small arms ammunition worldwide — and every handoff in a disposal chain is a point where the count can change.

TWB removes the transfers. Not one of them, all of them. The material is destroyed inside the perimeter of the organisation that owns it and never enters anyone else's hands.

Remote start, remote stop, armoured chamber, over-pressure relief, interlocked controls. The operator is never adjacent to live ordnance during a cycle. In July 2025 three Los Angeles County Sheriff's detectives were killed handling ordnance believed to be inert. That is the reason this machine exists.

Regulatory position

Not a cleaner way to open burn. Categorically not open burning.

Federal regulation defines open burning as combustion without control of combustion air, containment in an enclosed device, or control of gaseous emission products — 40 CFR 260.10. A closed, air-controlled chamber with emissions treatment does not meet that definition. That is regulatory language, not a marketing claim.

What we do not claim. EPA has proposed revisions to the standards for open burning and open detonation of waste explosives (89 FR 19952, March 2024) that would require facilities to evaluate and adopt alternatives where safe and available. That rule is proposed, not final. We describe it that way. We do not build a compliance case on a rule that has not issued.

What is already in force is the exception itself: open burning is permitted only for waste explosives that "cannot safely be disposed of through other modes of treatment." That conditional is where this technology lives.

The system

Vault

A closed, armoured, trailer-deployed destruction chamber for small arms ammunition up to .50 calibre.

Built to arrive behind a pickup truck.

Vault is a two-stage closed combustion system on a 26-foot trailer. It tows on a Class V hitch, runs on propane or diesel with an onboard generator, and needs a minimum standoff of 750 feet from occupied structures. It is designed around one constraint: that the organisation which owns the ammunition can destroy it without anyone else entering the process.

On throughput. Vault is rated at 60 lb/hr up to .50 calibre. We publish the hourly rate, not a daily figure — daily figures in this category assume shift lengths nobody runs. If your requirement is measured in tons per year, we are not the right supplier, and we will tell you so on the first call.

Recovery

Every round comes back as metal. None of it comes back as a part.

Combustion separates the round into its constituent metal streams, graded on site and ready for sale into existing secondary markets. Nothing recoverable leaves in a form that can be reassembled, resold as a component, or put back into circulation.

Lead & antimony

Cores recover as a Pb–Sb alloy ingot — battery-grade feedstock sought by secondary lead smelters.

Cartridge brass

Among the highest-value non-ferrous scrap grades, recovered clean and separated.

Copper jackets

Separated automatically within the same cycle. No downstream sorting required.

Verification status. Historic operating data indicates recovery of approximately 97% of recyclable material per burn cycle. Independent verification in progress

Third-party measurement of both recovery yield and stack emissions has been commissioned and the reports will be published. Until they exist we present these as our own operating figures rather than verified performance — and we would encourage you to hold every supplier in this category to that standard.

Chain of custody

The file closes without a gap in it.

Custody is not a feature of the machine. It is the reason to buy the machine.

What we commit to.

  • No transfer. The material does not leave your controlled area between the moment it is signed out and the moment it ceases to exist.
  • Your operators. We train your people. TWB personnel are not required for routine operation and are never custodians of your material.
  • No resale, no diversion. Recovered metal is graded and weighed on site under your observation. Nothing recovered from seized or surrendered property is resold as a component or a part.
  • Certificate of destruction. Issued per cycle against your own inventory identifiers, in a form built to attach directly to your property record.
  • Auditable. The protocol is written, versioned, and available for your internal audit or inspector general review before you buy anything.
Why this clause exists

The resale problem

In January 2024 the Michigan State Police suspended a firearms-destruction contract after it emerged that the contractor was reselling salvaged parts from destroyed weapons.

Destruction vendors who monetise recovered components have a structural conflict with the agencies they serve. We publish the no-resale commitment first, in writing, because it is the question a property custodian should ask and rarely gets answered.

Operators

If your name is on the property record, this is built for you.

Four kinds of organisation buy this machine, for four reasons. The custody argument is the same in all four.

Law enforcement

Evidence custodians · armourers · range officers · EOD

Seized ammunition. Surrendered rounds from buy-backs and take-back days. Duty ammunition past service life. Range sweepings. None of it can be sold, none of it can be discarded, and every round is signed for by somebody.

Vault clears the locker inside your own compound, on a schedule you set, with no contractor entering the chain. Usually procured on a blanket purchase order rather than a competition.

Commercial & industrial

Manufacturers · distributors · ranges · licensees

Off-specification production. Returned and expired stock. Discontinued lines. Ammunition that must be destroyed for legal or liability reasons and currently has to be shipped to a third party to do it.

Recovered metal offsets a meaningful share of disposal cost, and the material never leaves your facility or your insurance perimeter.

Defence — installation level

Range control · ammunition supply points · base EOD

The objection to closed disposal raised most often is that preprocessing and consolidation expose personnel to more handling risk, not less. That objection is correct — and it is an objection to fixed-site closed disposal.

A chamber that arrives at the point of storage removes the consolidation step entirely. The material is processed where it already sits, by people already cleared to handle it.

Allied & international

Partner-nation forces · stockpile reduction

Small arms ammunition is the stream alternative-technology programmes consistently score as out of scope. It is also among the most divertible material in any national stockpile, and the stream most often burned in the open for want of an alternative.

Deployed with allied and partner-nation forces across three continents. Nordic and Baltic distribution through Military Equipment Denmark A/S.

When we are the wrong answer.

Vault is a small arms system. If your requirement is artillery, bombs, rocket motors, bulk propellant, or destruction measured in tons per year, we are not competitive and we will say so on the first call rather than the third.

We would rather lose a bid we cannot serve than win one we cannot deliver. In this category a supplier who over-promises creates a safety problem, not just a commercial one.
