The problem

Every transfer is a window. Most disposal chains are made of them.

The standard method for destroying ammunition in 2026 is to put it in a hole in the ground and set it on fire. The standard alternative is to hand it to a contractor and take their word for it.

Both routes move the material out of your control. A manifest is written. A truck leaves. Somebody else’s people handle rounds that are still, on paper, your responsibility. Poorly secured and surplus stockpiles are a documented source of diverted small arms ammunition worldwide — and every handoff in a disposal chain is a point where the count can change.

TWB removes the transfers. Not one of them, all of them. The material is destroyed inside the perimeter of the organisation that owns it and never enters anyone else’s hands.

02

Zero transfers. Zero diversion windows.

Signed out once, destroyed, weighed, certified. No manifest, no transport leg, no third-party handling, no interval where the material is somewhere you are not. The chain of custody does not have a gap in it because there is no gap to have.

03

Recovered, not released.

Combustion separates the round into graded lead, antimony, brass and copper on your pad. Nothing usable leaves as a component. What comes out is a domestic supply of critical metals — recovered at the point of disposal instead of buried in a burn pit or sold on as parts. And it is the one line of the job that pays back: the metal sells into secondary markets that already exist, and that value comes off the cost of destruction.

04

Nobody stands over it.

Remote start, remote stop, armoured chamber, over-pressure relief, interlocked controls. The operator is never adjacent to live ordnance during a cycle. In July 2025 three Los Angeles County Sheriff’s detectives were killed handling ordnance believed to be inert. That is the reason this machine exists.

Cost recovery

Every other route is pure cost. This one ends in graded metal.

Contracted destruction is priced by the ton — GAO put conventional demilitarization near $2,000 a ton in FY15, and the National Academies’ average runs $2,890 — and at the end of it you hold a receipt. At the end of a Vault cycle you hold lead, antimony, brass and copper, weighed on your own scale.

The cores come back as battery-grade Pb–Sb alloy. Cartridge brass is among the highest-value non-ferrous scrap grades. Copper jackets separate in the same cycle. All of it sells into markets that already exist, which changes what destruction is on a budget line: not a fee paid to lose material, but a cost with material coming back against it.

Score every bid in this category on the net cost of destruction — the price minus the verified value of what you get back. It is a question only one supplier wants you to ask.

What we will not quote. Our operating figure is approximately 97% of recyclable material recovered per cycle — held as our own number until commissioned third-party measurement publishes, and we will not put a dollar recovery table in front of you before then. If raw price per ton is the whole decision, a burn pit wins it; that is exactly the pricing that regulation is closing down. The comparison that matters is against compliant, contracted disposal — and that is the one we invite.

Regulatory position

Not a cleaner way to open burn. Categorically not open burning.

Federal regulation defines open burning as combustion without control of combustion air, containment in an enclosed device, or control of gaseous emission products — 40 CFR 260.10. A closed, air-controlled chamber with emissions treatment does not meet that definition. That is regulatory language, not a marketing claim.

What we do not claim. EPA has proposed revisions to the standards for open burning and open detonation of waste explosives (89 FR 19952, March 2024) that would require facilities to evaluate and adopt alternatives where safe and available. That rule is proposed, not final. We describe it that way. We do not build a compliance case on a rule that has not issued.

What is already in force is the exception itself: open burning is permitted only for waste explosives that “cannot safely be disposed of through other modes of treatment.” That conditional is where this technology lives.
