The one-line read. More armed conflicts than at any point since 1945, more of them between states, and the money is all flowing into making munitions — with no published policy or funding line anywhere for destroying surplus. That asymmetry, not any single war, is the market.
Europe & Eurasia

Ukraine — no ceasefire; July was the deadliest month for civilians since May 2022 (437 killed, OHCHR, 12 Aug). A joint Ukraine–US–Europe peace proposal was floated ~22 Aug and rejected. NATO's eastern flank — a Russian cruise missile detonated in eastern Poland on 30 Jul; drones crossed into Moldova and Romania on 20 Aug. No casualties yet, which is the only reason there has been no Article 4. Türkiye–PKK — parliament passed the reintegration law 10 Aug, 468–88, but it is inert until the National Security Council certifies full weapons surrender, and no destruction or verification mechanism has been named. That is the single most direct opening in this edition. Moldova — the Cobasna depot, up to 20,000 t of Soviet ammunition, remains OSCE-unmonitored with no disposal programme of any kind.

Middle East & North Africa

Gaza — the Board of Peace decommissioning agreement (30–31 Jul) provides for Hamas weapons to be inventoried and stored under a Palestinian committee, not destroyed and not handed to Israel; sequencing is deadlocked. 589 explosive-ordnance incidents since Oct 2023 have killed 274 and injured 1,142; 61m tonnes of contaminated rubble. Iran/Gulf — the 60-day negotiating window expired 17 Aug with no deal and Hormuz effectively closed. Iran fired 6,700+ munitions at the GCC between February and May; no state has published any clearance accounting for what came down. Lebanon — ceasefire violated continuously; UNIFIL terminates 31 Dec and nothing has been named to inherit EOD south of the Litani. Iraq — the reported September weapons-surrender deadline was publicly disavowed by the government on 21–24 Aug; I pulled it from the brief at verification.

Africa

Sudan remains the deadliest war in the world — 200,000+ newly displaced in Kordofan on 20 Aug, and UNMAS has detected new minefields being laid into central Khartoum return areas. South Sudan is back at open war, and carries the benchmark number in this brief: UNMAS has destroyed 7.3 million small-arms ammunition items there, funded through UN assessed contributions — proof that a recurring institutional buyer for exactly our category exists. DRC tabled an FDLR DDR plan on 13 Aug; first ceasefire verification ran 24 Aug. Somalia — AUSSOM's drawdown was accelerated by 12–18 months on 31 Jul, which compresses base and ammunition handovers to the Somali army with no stockpile-security funding attached. Depot failures — Burundi (13 killed, April) and three others in seven months, none of them yet in the Small Arms Survey dataset, which stops at December 2024.

Asia-Pacific & the Americas

Myanmar is the world's second-worst country for mine casualties (745 in 2025) with no DDR mechanism at all. Philippines — MILF decommissioning is Asia's largest weapons-handover programme and it is frozen; the BARMM elections on 14 September are the forcing event. Thailand–Cambodia — the December ceasefire carries an explicit joint demining obligation across ~800 km that is politically stalled while casualties continue. Taiwan — read the halved PLA sortie count as a change of method, not de-escalation; coast guard activity is up 120%. Americas — this is a seized-property problem, not a battlefield-ordnance one, and it is the closest analogue to our existing law-enforcement work.
