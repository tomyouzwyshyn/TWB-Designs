01 — The verdict

There is a good business in here. It is not the one on the slides.

What is genuinely strong

  • Twenty-five years in service, inside other people's perimeters. Police forces and armies on three continents let an energetic process run inside their fence line. Nobody does that on the strength of a pitch. If the incident log is clean, it is the single highest-value asset the company owns — and the deck does not mention it once.
  • The custody wedge is real and uncontested. Every competitor either moves the material or takes three weeks to arrive. That is now sourceable: Dynasafe's own SDC datasheet states the mobile SDC 1000 deploys in three weeks. The competitor published the number your claim depends on.
  • The entry price. A permitted, referenced, debt-free 25-year platform acquired at ~1.2× book and under 3× EBITDA, on a retiring-founder catalyst. That is precisely the shape a lower-mid-market sponsor hunts for.
  • The supporting memo. Section 2 discloses the real baseline; the discount-period analysis refuses the shortcut that would have printed $845M instead of $236M. That is the work of someone who did not want to fool himself. Keep it — but move it into the deck.

Where the platform thesis breaks

  • The deck's own throughput number refutes its own market slide. At 1,000 lb/day one unit does 125 short tons a year. Destroying 1,000 tons takes 2,000 machine-days. The market slides are sized in billions against stockpiles measured in hundreds of thousands of tons.
  • The US Army demil programme is not the market — and cannot be. The live solicitation is coded NAICS 332993, "Ammunition, except Small Arms." Its six CLIN groups are 30mm DU, ICM, 20mm, HC smoke, flares, MLRS rocket motors. Not one is inside a .50 cal ceiling.
  • Every year of the roadmap after 2027 walks away from the wedge into throughput markets where the brand platform already concedes TWB loses by 3× to 200×.
  • Two unsigned institutional endorsements carry the whole bridge between the two — and one of them names a university that does not work in the field.
The contradiction underneath everything

Your brand platform locks the rule "never let a comparison be scored on throughput; reframe to rounds destroyed without leaving the compound." The investor deck then builds its market slide on tonnage-derived dollars. Both documents cannot be right. The brand platform is the one that survives contact with a buyer.

02 — Ranked

Five things that will kill the raise if they are not fixed

Ordered by how fast a partner finds them. The first three are findable in a single search from a government PDF.

  1. 01

    The Army numbers are wrong by roughly ten times, and the correct number is public

    The deck says the Army has "committed over $3bn across its 2026–27 demil awards." That figure is $808.8M + $2.30B added together. Both components fail. The actual line is $155.1M enacted FY26 and $161.2M requested FY27 — Exhibit P-1, Procurement of Ammunition Army, Production Base Support, line 36, published by the DoW Comptroller in April 2026.

    This is not a sizing-methodology dispute. It is a number that is simply not the number, and it sits in a PDF any analyst downloads in one search.

  2. 02

    Hawthorne is not a proof point — it is the strongest counter-argument in the deck

    The $2.30B award to Day & Zimmermann is a 20-year base-operations contract running to December 2046, in which demilitarization is one of fifteen work statements. Worse: PWS 2 §5.1.13 explicitly includes small arms ammunition, permits OB/OD at designated ranges, and instructs the contractor to price net of "anticipated proceeds from recovered materials and scrap."

    That is TWB's exact cost-recovery pitch, written into a competitor's twenty-year contract, executable by open burning at ~$750/ton. Citing Hawthorne invites the reader to discover the Army just locked its largest small-arms demil venue away until 2046.

  3. 03

    The plan consumes more capital than the deck says the company is worth

    Seven to ten acquisitions entering at $15–20M of revenue, at realistic lower-middle-market prices, is $150–350M of purchase consideration before fees, integration and working capital. The deck's own implied fair value for the entire company is $235.9M. There is no ask, no use of proceeds, no sources and uses, no dilution schedule and no integration function anywhere in twenty-one slides.

    The roll-up thesis and the valuation cannot both be true. A plan that names its own capital requirement reads as competent; one that does not reads as naive.

  4. 04

    Two unsigned endorsements carry Years 1 and 2 — and one names the wrong institution

    "Licensed from a U.S. university (name TBC)" tells a reader you have not called a technology transfer office, have no NDA, no term sheet, and no field-of-use price. And MIT is not merely one option among several — it is the wrong institution. Electromagnetic-induction metals discrimination is a twenty-year SERDP/ESTCP community: NRL, Berkeley Lab, Geometrics, White River, Dartmouth Thayer (Shubitidze), Duke (Collins). MIT has no lab, no PI and no programme in it.

    Naming MIT in a document soliciting investment, with no executed agreement, is not a credibility problem. It is a disclosure problem.

  5. 05

    The chemical weapons line signals that nobody checked

    OPCW confirmed on 7 July 2023 that all 72,304.34 tonnes of declared chemical agent worldwide had been verifiably destroyed. The US was the last possessor state to finish. The appropriation that paid for it went $1,090M (FY24) → $776M (FY25) → $213M (FY26 request), and PEO ACWA's own site states that once closure concludes the programme will cease to exist.

    There is no stockpile left to bid on in 2036. The inference a reader draws from this slide then contaminates every other item on the roadmap.

04 — Competition

The competitive landscape the deck leaves blank

Almost every player is private, so this is built from contract awards, solicitations, registry filings, insolvency records and trade press. Tiering is by collision with TWB's actual position, not by size.

Tier 1 — Direct collision

Three companies sell what TWB sells, to the buyer TWB sells to

EnviroSafe Demil LLC

Hooks, TX · private, Banyan Growth Partners · founded 2018

This is not "a competitor already identified." It is the same company with a US passport. Primary NAICS is 332992 — Small Arms Ammunition, TWB's exact category. Markets destruction to .50 cal, offers to deploy mobile systems to the client site, and makes the same "typically require no permits" argument. Self-certified Small Disadvantaged Business with a CAGE code, SAM registration and a seat on the Rock Island IDIQ — every structural advantage the Canadian entity legally cannot hold. Has already built the $20–25M fixed-site backbone at TexAmericas that TWB's Years 1–3 roadmap describes building.

The unknown that decides everything: their actual mobile deployment lead time is undisclosed. Until someone establishes it, "same day, on site" is a question worth asking, not a claim worth making.

Arcwood Environmental (formerly Heritage / EBV Explosives)

Indianapolis · EQT Infrastructure majority · demil site Joplin, MO

Not previously on the list, and it should have been. Las Vegas · ownership undisclosed

The sharpest new finding, and nowhere in the deck. Set that against an 11,500 lb trailer at 60 lb/hr. Adding an energetics front end does not scale Vault — it replaces Vault with a multi-container, ten-day-mobilisation, fifteen-crew plant, and in doing so destroys the custody wedge. You can no longer roll into a police compound in the morning and be done by afternoon. The wedge and the roadmap are in direct conflict, and the deck does not say so.

06 — Capital

The acquisition thesis, priced honestly

You asked to leave the raise amount open. That is fine. The cost of the plan is not optional — a PE reader prices it whether you show it or not.

What the model assumes

  • 7 acquisitions, $18M average revenue at entry, 15%/yr post-acquisition growth
  • Deal spend priced at 1.2× revenue — $151.2M cumulative for $465M of acquired revenue
  • Majority of Y10 revenue from acquired businesses
  • 20% terminal EBITDA margin

What the transaction record shows

  • Cadre — your own comp — disproves your arithmetic. Since its 2021 IPO it deployed ~$455M across seven deals to move revenue from $427.3M to $610.3M. That is ~$2.50 of capital per $1.00 of acquired revenue, with 3–5% organic growth underneath.
  • The strongest disclosed comp is 7.1×, not 19×: Cadre paid £75M for Carr's Engineering on £51.1m revenue and £7.8m adjusted operating profit — a licensed, IP-rich nuclear remote-handling group ten times TWB's size.
  • GF Data 2025: lower-middle-market averages 7.2× EBITDA, sub-$10M EV deals ~5.5×.
  • Honest frame: buy at 5.5–7.5×, exit a services platform at 9–12×. That arbitrage is the entire investment case and it should be the headline, not the residual.
The number the deck has to name

There is no version of this plan that does not require $150M+ of committed acquisition capital by Year 4, and the three independent analyses landed at $150–350M depending on entry multiple. Against a $700K entry price and $863K of current revenue, that is the fact the deck has to confront rather than omit. It also settles the structure question: this is a sponsored platform or it is nothing. Every precedent found — SilverEdge/Godspeed, Venterra/General Atlantic, Cadre/Kanders — required an institutional sponsor from the outset and an operating chairman who had closed deals before.

Three execution problems the model assumes away
  • Set-aside destruction. The principal asset of most acquirable US targets is small-business, 8(a), NHO or ESOP status and the vehicles it unlocks — and that status is extinguished at closing by sale to a foreign-owned parent. This is not a footnote; it is the central execution problem.
  • You are late, not early. EQT took Arcwood and Arcwood took EBV out of General Dynamics. Diehl took EST Energetics. Rheinmetall absorbed Expal. Cadre took ICOR. General Atlantic and First Reserve put ~£150M into Venterra. TWB would be bidding against prime balance sheets for assets whose realistic profile is 60–65 staff and financial distress — not $18M of clean revenue.
  • The targets are smaller than modelled. Zapata's entire munitions response division — 25 years of USACE past performance, 40+ contracts — was twenty people when it sold. EST Energetics is just over 60.

The outcome that is actually fundable

If the acquisition capital is not raised, the credible ten-year outcome for a well-run TWB is a $30–80M revenue specialist with a genuine custody moat and a defensible 8–11× exit — a $100–250M enterprise value, achieved without diluting the founder to a sliver. That is a good outcome and it is fundable this quarter.

The $450–500M version is fundable only by someone who is not the current shareholder base, on terms that leave the current shareholder base with very little.

The opportunity the deck misses entirely

Germany's Emergency Programme for Unexploded Ordnance in the North Sea and Baltic is funded at €100M, has contracted recovery in the Bay of Lübeck, and its explicitly stated next phase is a floating munitions-disposal facility that destroys recovered ordnance near the dump site so it never has to be brought ashore.

That is TWB's custody wedge, stated by the buyer, in the buyer's own words, backed by appropriated money. The recovered material is dominated by small-calibre — the 2024 pilot brought up ~10 tonnes of smaller-calibre including 5.8 tonnes of 20mm. ~1.6 million tonnes are dumped in German waters alone. It reaches you through the existing Nordic–Baltic relationship, not through a chemical weapons slide.
