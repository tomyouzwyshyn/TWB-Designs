01
The read

The technology is real. The story was aimed at the wrong market.

03
The wedge

Custody is the proof point. Everything else is a feature.

Reframed 2026-09-01: custody is no longer the category, it is the reason eMACS wins the segment it already operates in. Ammunition awaiting destruction is not waste. It is a liability with a named person’s signature on it — and every existing solution requires that liability to travel.

Think about what actually happens today. A property room accumulates seized and surrendered ammunition. It cannot be sold. It cannot be thrown away. It degrades. It is signed for. And the only lawful routes out of the building all involve handing it to someone else: a contractor picks it up, a depot receives it, a range officer takes it to a pit and sets it on fire.

Every one of those routes creates a manifest, a transport risk, a diversion opportunity, and a gap in the chain of custody where a person’s name is on a document but the material is in someone else’s truck. PIKA ships it to a facility. EnviroSafe ships it to Texas. CADS, TWB’s own sister company, picks it up and takes it away.

TWB is the only answer where the ammunition is destroyed inside the perimeter of the organisation that owns it, by that organisation’s own people, and never enters anyone else’s custody at all.

The positioning, in one sentence

This reframes every specification into a custody argument, and it reframes the competitive set into a single question the customer already worries about.

What the spec says

  • Class V hitch, 11,500 lb, 26-foot trailer
  • Remote start/stop, interlocked
  • Two operators
  • 750 ft standoff
  • 97% recoverable material

What it means

  • One truck brings it. No mobilisation project.
  • Nobody stands over live ordnance.
  • Your people, not a cleared contractor.
  • It fits inside a compound you already control.
  • What comes out is metal, not a disposal receipt.

The competitive proof for this is unusually clean. Dynasafe’s mobile Static Detonation Chamber — the closest true competitor, and the category’s brand leader — states on its own datasheet that it deploys in three weeks. That is the fight to pick, and it is the only fight on this list TWB wins outright.

Messaging

Order matters more than wording. Each claim is ranked by how contested it is — lead with what nobody else can say.

Same day. On site. Your own people. Uncontested

The only genuinely unclaimed position in the category, and it is true. Everyone else’s “mobile” means a mobilisation project measured in weeks. Own speed-to-first-burn. Nobody is defending it.

Every competitor moves the material. GunBusters built a national law-enforcement business on exactly this promise — for firearms — and left ammunition untouched. CADS already has the audit-trail language buried in a footer; promote it to a promise and put a certificate of destruction behind it.

Not an environmental benefit — a domestic supply of critical metals recovered at the point of disposal. This is the Redwood Materials move, and nobody in demil has made it. 4

Because nobody should have to do this by hand. Nobody is using it

On 18 July 2025 three Los Angeles County Sheriff’s Department detectives were killed handling ordnance believed to be inert — the deadliest day for that department since 1857. Every competitor treats safety as a compliance argument. It is a moral argument, and it belongs to whoever says it first.

The indicting comparison

Anduril’s single most effective rhetorical device is the sentence that makes the status quo look absurd by setting it beside something ordinary — “there is more AI in a Tesla than in any U.S. military vehicle.” TWB’s version is available and nobody has taken it:

In 2026, the standard method for destroying ammunition is to put it in a hole in the ground and set it on fire.

Verifiable — Tooele Army Depot was still open-detonating as of June 2025

The defensive rule — revised 2026-09-01

On eMACS specifically, don’t let a conversation be scored on pounds per day, tons per year, or net explosive quantity without a second sentence. Give the metric eMACS wins first, then name the acquisition programme as the answer to tonnage rather than declining the conversation:

05
Audiences

One spine, four proofs.

The custody argument holds for every buyer. What changes is which proof you lead with — and how fast the door opens.

Priority 1 · Reachable now

State, provincial & municipal law enforcement

Who: evidence and property custodians, armourers, range officers, EOD teams. Lead with: custody and operator safety. Proof: six RCMP units, Sûreté du Québec, Calgary, Winnipeg, Franklin County OH — an excellent reference list currently going completely unused.

Buying looks like a blanket purchase order, not a competition. Small per transaction, very high in count. Do not put a market size on this segment — no public data exists, and inventing one is how a credible brief gets discredited.

Priority 2 · Reachable now

Commercial & industrial

Who: ammunition manufacturers with off-spec product, distributors, ranges, the 9,309 federal explosives licensees ATF counts. Lead with: cost recovery and no-transfer liability. Proof: Federal Cartridge Company.

The US consumed roughly 3.2 billion pounds of explosives in 2022 by ATF’s own count. Even a fractional off-spec rate against that base exceeds the entire DoD demil stockpile. It is the best-sourced commercial anchor available.

Priority 3 · Build toward

US federal & DoD

Who: installation and depot level first, not the demil program office. Lead with: safety of handling — not environmental compliance. This matters: Joint Munitions Command publicly defends open burning, and its stated first objection to closed disposal is that preprocessing “exposes personnel to more explosive safety hazards.”

A mobile chamber that processes at the point of storage answers that objection directly. Arguing environment puts TWB in a fight the Army has already rehearsed and won. Arguing safety fights on ground the Army chose.

Priority 4 · Qualify carefully

Allied, NATO & humanitarian

Who: NSPA, allied MoDs, UNMAS, the US State Department’s weapons-removal programme, HALO and NPA. Existing footprint: Australia, New Zealand, Saudi Arabia, Dubai Police, Nordics and Baltics via Military Equipment Denmark.

Read this before pursuing UNMAS. Their 2025 mobile-destruction tenders specified 40 kg/hr minimum, 20-foot ISO container, and mechanical rather than incineration processes. TWB fails all three. That is either a disqualifier or the sharpest product-roadmap brief available — but it is not a market to claim in a deck.

Two claims to build the narrative on instead

The throughput collapse

Joint Munitions Command averaged 48,000 short tons a year from 2015 to 2020 and set a target of getting the stockpile below 100,000 tons. The FY26–31 plan programs 22,000–24,000 tons a year, and the goal has disappeared from the published strategy while the forecast shows growth every year through FY2030.

Throughput has halved, the target is gone, and the pile is growing. Every number in that sentence comes from a government source.

The definitional gift

Federal regulation at 40 CFR 260.10 defines open burning as combustion without control of combustion air, containment in an enclosed device, or control of gaseous emissions. A closed chamber with controlled air and emissions control is therefore, as a matter of regulatory definition, categorically not open burning.

That is a citable fact, not a marketing claim — and it is far more durable than any competitor’s “clean alternative” tagline.

10
Risk

Three things that could break this.

Named plainly, because each one is manageable now and expensive later.

The regulatory classification trap Highest consequence

“Two-stage combustion” is the phrase TWB currently leads with. Under 40 CFR 260.10, an incinerator is any enclosed device using controlled flame combustion that is not a boiler or industrial furnace. A miscellaneous unit under Subpart X is expressly one that does not fit the incinerator category.

On the face of those definitions, a two-stage combustion chamber is an incinerator — which pulls in a substantially heavier permitting regime than the Subpart X route a detonation chamber can argue for. Resolve this with environmental counsel before the language is locked into a website, because the words TWB uses to describe the process may determine which regime applies. It is also a reason not to lead with the mechanism at all: lead with custody, describe the mechanism precisely and only where required.

The US standing gap

The Hamilton entity can hold a SAM.gov registration, a UEI and an NCAGE. It cannot hold a US CAGE code, any small-business or set-aside status, an ATF explosives licence, or a DDTC registration. Publishing any of those before the US entity exists is a size-and-status misrepresentation with False Claims Act exposure — there is no upside that justifies it.

The genuinely good news, and it is underused: Canada is a DFARS qualifying country. Canadian end products are exempt from the Buy American statute in DoD acquisitions, and the Canadian Commercial Corporation provides a government-backed prime contracting route. Say that in those exact words. It is checkable, it is a real advantage, and almost no foreign supplier claims it properly.

EnviroSafe Demil

A US company, formed 2018, sitting on a former Army ammunition plant in Texas, holding a share of the Army’s demil contract vehicle and self-certified as a small disadvantaged business. Their tagline is “America’s clean alternative to open burning.” They claim .50 calibre, mobile deployment, and materials recovery.

They occupy, almost verbatim, four of the five things TWB would want to say — plus the one thing TWB does not have, which is a US contract vehicle. This is precisely why the positioning must move to custody and speed. What EnviroSafe does not have: throughput figures, named customers, named leadership, published permits, or a machine. They sell “send it to us.” TWB sells “it never leaves.” That distinction is the whole business.

What we took from Shield AI, and what we left

Taken — the mechanics

  • Short declaratives. One idea per sentence, no subordinate clause softening the claim.
  • Verb-first construction and absolute quantities — zero transfers, not minimal handling.
  • Hard claims backed immediately by a checkable number or a named source.
  • No hedging vocabulary. Nothing is “designed to help” or “can assist with”.
  • Mission stated as protection, not capability. Shield’s own line is “protect service members and civilians” — much closer to TWB’s register than their visual language suggests.

Left — the targeting vocabulary

  • No hunting, dominance, lethality or battlefield framing. TWB destroys ammunition for sheriffs and armourers; that language misfires with the buyer reachable this year.
  • No product-as-weapon naming. eMACS holds a perimeter — it does not prosecute a target.
  • Adopting the offense voice would drop TWB into the crowded lane the strategy deliberately avoids. The look can be hard; the claim stays protective.

Threat framing — the sharpened line

The custody argument gets materially stronger when the consequence is named rather than implied. Custody is not paperwork hygiene; it is diversion control. Every handoff in a disposal chain is an interval where the count can change, and poorly secured or surplus stockpiles are a documented source of diverted small arms ammunition worldwide.

Every transfer is a window. Most disposal chains are made of them. TWB removes the transfers — not one of them, all of them.

The threat-framed version of the wedge

The discipline that keeps this credible: threat framing must stay evidenced. “Diverted stockpiles are a documented source” is defensible and sourced. “Terrorists are buying out of your evidence room” is not, and one unsupported line of it would cost more than the whole register gains. Name the mechanism, cite the source, stop.

Rules

  • Earn every claim with a number or drop the sentence. This category punishes hand-waving harder than most.
  • Concrete nouns beat abstract ones. Brass, lead, trailer, hitch, locker, perimeter — not solutions, capabilities, offerings.
  • Write to the person holding the liability, not the person approving the budget. The budget-holder reads over their shoulder.
  • Name the gate on every roadmap claim. Precision reads as competence.
  • Dual-audience test: if a line sounds like hype to a uniformed evaluator or to a diligence lead, cut it.

Banned

  • Solutions, offerings, capabilities — used by every operator in the category, meaning nothing.
  • Environmentally friendly, green, sustainable — soft, defensive, and claimed by literally everyone including TWB today.
  • R3, resource recovery and reclamation — insider jargon, and already taken by Nammo and Gradient.
  • World leader, world experts — El Dorado owns this verbatim and it is unprovable anyway.
  • Warfighter, lethality, arsenal — wrong register. TWB’s mission is making things safe, and that is the differentiated voice.
  • Clean alternative to open burning — three competitors deep. Use the regulatory definition instead.

Typography

A single-face system, worked light. Headings run 400 at display size, 500 at section level, 600 at sub-head — weight climbing as size falls, so the scale carries the hierarchy and weight only assists. Body at 400. Headings track tight and loosen as they get smaller. Monospace, uppercase, letter-spaced for the whole UI layer: navigation, labels, buttons, specifications, status.

The restraint is the point. One text face plus one mono reads as a document produced by an organisation with a standard, not a brand reaching for personality — which is the correct register for procurement officers and evidence custodians. It also carries no commercial font licence: Helvetica ships on macOS and resolves to Arial elsewhere with identical metrics.

Photography

The trailer in real compounds, in daylight, with people in ordinary work clothing. No dramatic fire imagery — fire is what the competition does in a pit. Show the output: graded metal in bins. That single image carries claim 3 without a caption.

Motion

Restrained and mechanical. Scroll reveal at 14px translate over 0.62s, staggered in 70ms steps. Buttons transition in 0.12s on cubic-bezier(.4,0,.2,1), brackets scaling outward 2.8% on hover. Nothing bounces, nothing floats, and all of it disables under prefers-reduced-motion.

The CTA, specified: zero border radius, 1px hairline border at 14% black, 2.8% black fill, mono uppercase at 0.72rem on 0.09em tracking, and four 6×6px corner brackets set 3px outside the box on two adjacent sides each. On hover the fill flips to sage, the type goes white, and the brackets push outward. That one component carries most of the borrowed character.

The logo should read as a mark on equipment before it reads as a mark on a website — this brand gets seen on a trailer in a compound more often than on a screen. Stencil-legible at low contrast, single colour, no gradient, works at 40mm on painted steel.

Proof

What has to exist before the brand can carry weight.

Positioning is a promise. In this category the promise is checked. These are the assets that turn the strategy into something an evaluator can verify.

Build first — no US entity required

  • Third-party emissions testing at an accredited lab, with a publishable report. This retires the softest claim in the offer.
  • Independent verification of the 97% recovery figure.
  • A written chain-of-custody and certificate-of-destruction protocol — independently auditable. This is the product as much as the trailer is.
  • Named patent numbers, and filings where gaps exist. The strongest sole-source lever available.
