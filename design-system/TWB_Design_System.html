The system,
in one page.

Every asset TWB produces — site, deck, capability statement, one-pager, trailer livery — is built from what is on this page. If a value is not here, it is not in the system.

01
Colour

One accent. Everything else is black, grey, or white.

The rule that keeps sage visible

An accent that is only bound to labels and hairlines is invisible in practice, whatever its hex. Sage must own whole surfaces, not just marks. Every section that carries material, recovery, specification or evidence sits on the sage wash with sage hairlines — hero proof strip, track record, recovery, spec tables, capability cards. If a page has proof on it, that proof is visibly green.

Sage is the only hue in the system. It carries action, material and proof alike — buttons on hover, section numbers, labels, recovered metal, track record, specifications. There is no second accent competing with it.

The negative register — conventional disposal, superseded claims, the thing being argued against — is carried by weight and form, not colour: a dark neutral with dashed strokes against solid sage. Inert against live. That reads more precisely than red-versus-green, and it avoids a traffic-light palette in front of buyers who see enough of those.

02
Typography

One text face, worked light. Scale does more than weight.

Headings run 400 at display size, 500 at section level, 600 at sub-head — the weight climbing as the size falls, because large type carries on its own and small type does not. Body sits at 400 with a slight negative track (−.008em) — Helvetica Neue sets a touch loose at text size and tightens well. The scale does the hierarchy; weight only assists. Headings track tight (−.028em at display, easing to −.009em) because a grotesque tightens as it grows.

Helvetica Neue leads the font stack. Plain Helvetica and Arial ship only Regular and Bold, so a request for 500 or 600 would snap to one or the other. Neue carries the intermediate weights on macOS; elsewhere the design degrades to 400/700 and still holds, just with less finesse in the middle.

IBM Plex Mono uppercase for the entire UI layer — navigation, labels, buttons, specifications, status. This is the only place a second face appears, and it is doing a different job: the mono register is what makes a page read as an instrument rather than a brochure.

Helvetica ships on macOS and resolves to Arial elsewhere — metrically identical, so layouts hold exactly. Nothing in this system requires a commercial font licence.

04
Motion

Mechanical, not expressive.

Nothing bounces, nothing floats, nothing eases out slowly. Movement in this system reads as a mechanism actuating — short, linear-ish, and over before you notice it.

05
Applying it

Where each value goes, on any new asset.

Digital — site, deck, documents

  • White ground. Black headings at 900. Mono for every label, spec and status.
  • Sage for the one thing you want acted on per screen. More than one and it stops meaning anything.
  • Sage for material and proof. Brackets on cards that carry evidence.
  • Hairlines at 1px #E2E1DA. No shadows, no rounded corners, no gradients other than the hero wash.

Physical — trailer, equipment, print

  • The mark must read single-colour at 40mm on painted steel. No gradient, no fine detail.
  • On equipment the ground inverts: white or sage mark on the machine’s own dark finish. The system tolerates this; the digital surfaces do not.
  • Corner brackets scale as a physical registration mark on panels, plates and case labels.
  • Statutory hazard marking is the one place an outside colour is allowed — regulatory red and yellow are prescribed by safety convention and are not part of this system. Never restyle them to match the brand.

Threat framing is an asset only while every instance of it is checkable. Name the mechanism, cite the source, stop. One unsupported line costs more than the whole register gains.

Font availability. Helvetica is a system face on macOS and requires no licence for web or print. On Windows and most Linux it resolves to Arial or Liberation Sans, which are metrically identical — layouts hold exactly, with only minor letterform differences (notably R, G and the terminals on C and S). Only IBM Plex Mono is web-served, from Google Fonts.

A note on naming. There is no sans-serif Times — Times is defined by its serifs and Linotype never issued a sans companion. A knockoff family named “Times Sans Serif” circulates in some font packs; it is a Helvetica derivative and should not be used. Specify Helvetica.
